# Movement Stimulation through Social Media: The Tweeted Perspective and Road Safety Movement in Bangladesh

# Bachelor Thesis at Daffodil International University (BSc in Software Engineering)

**Published in:** 2021 4th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Big Data (ICAIBD)
**DOI:** [10.1109/ICAIBD51990.2021.9459042](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICAIBD51990.2021.9459042)
**Pages:** 584–588

## Abstract

This study examines how social media, particularly Twitter, amplifies real-world protest movements, using Bangladesh's 2018 Road Safety Movement as a case study. The authors compare front-page newspaper coverage from five national dailies against Twitter hashtag activity collected via the Brand24 analytics tool, aiming to determine whether hashtag momentum on Twitter correlates with — and helps drive — the intensity of on-the-ground protest.

## Keywords

`Twitter data analysis` · `road safety movement` · `Bangladesh` · `hashtags` · `movement stimulation` · `social media impact`

## Background

The Road Safety Movement began on July 29, 2018, after two students were killed by a speeding bus in Dhaka. It rapidly grew into a nationwide protest — led largely by school and university students — demanding safer roads across Bangladesh. The paper situates this movement alongside other social-media-fueled uprisings (e.g., the Arab Spring) to frame its central question: **does Twitter hashtag activity meaningfully track — and potentially drive — the scale of ground protest?**

## Methodology

The research follows a comparative, two-source data collection design:

1. **Newspaper data** — Front-page coverage (article count and column space) from five reputed Bangladeshi dailies: *Prothom Alo, Jugantor, Ittefaq, Vorer Kagoj,* and *The Daily Star*, tracked daily from July 29 to August 9, 2018.
2. **Twitter data** — Hashtag mention counts and social-media "reach" (shares), collected via the **Brand24** social monitoring tool, for the seven major protest hashtags (e.g., `#RoadSafetyMovement`, `#WeDemandJustice`, `#BangladeshStudentProtest`).
3. **Correlation analysis** — Since newspapers typically report on the previous day's events, each day's Twitter activity was compared against the *following* day's newspaper coverage to assess directional influence.

A working-process diagram (Figure 1 in the paper) outlines the full pipeline: newspaper coverage counting → hashtag selection and mention/share tracking → comparative analysis → conclusion.

## Key Findings

- Newspaper front-page coverage rose from **0% on July 29** (protest's first day) to **100% coverage by August 2–7**, as the protest intensified and turned violent.
- Twitter hashtag mentions and reach generally **rose in step with newspaper coverage**, peaking around August 5–7 alongside real-world escalation (police action against students, the Prime Minister's public statements).
- The relationship wasn't perfectly linear: a few notable exceptions occurred (e.g., August 1 coverage dipped despite rising Twitter activity, attributed to competing national news; August 3 fell on a public holiday, reducing engagement).
- Coverage and hashtag activity both declined by August 8–9 as government statements and new regulations began de-escalating the protest.
- Overall conclusion: **ground protest and Twitter activity moved largely in parallel**, supporting the idea that social media meaningfully stimulates and sustains real-world movements — and, notably, does so without geographic boundaries.
